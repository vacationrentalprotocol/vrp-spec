ADR 0012: The Stay Asked For, Real Dates, Echoed Fields and Offer Lifetime

Status: Proposed Date: 2026-10-17 (proposed)

Context

A signed offer proves that the host domain's key signed exactly these terms (§5). On top of ADR 0009 (head 3691630 of its pull request), spec/v0.1.md did not say what those terms must be for in five places:

  1. The stay asked for. §7 listed what makes an offer safe to quote, but nothing tied the signed offer to the dates and guest count the agent asked for. A genuine, fresh offer for other dates or another guest count met every condition. In the normative sections request appears only in §5's list of required members and in §5.1, which says the booking URL's checkIn, checkOut and guests match it; §15 (informative) says offers echo it.
  2. Dates. Nothing required request.check_in and request.check_out to be dates that exist, check_out to be later than check_in, or check_in not to have passed. The schema's date pattern ^[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}$ accepts 2026-13-01 and 2026-02-30, and a JavaScript Date turns 2027-02-30T00:00:00Z into 2 March 2027 instead of rejecting it.
  3. Echoed fields. Everything inside the JWS is signed, so whatever a node copies from the request becomes a host-signed value. Nothing limited what may be copied. The schema typed request.language as any string, so free text from a requester could be signed by the host's key.
  4. Lifetime. §3.1 spoke of "the maximum offer lifetime" without defining it. No rule limited valid_until − generated_at, and none kept generated_at from lying in the future, so an offer could stay quotable for years. The known production node uses 900 seconds (12 of 12 offers fetched 2026-10-17 between 10:49 and 16:12 UTC); the repository's examples use 600.
  5. Schema. The schema did not require request, so an offer without one was schema-valid although §7 now has to compare it. The same holds for node_id and property, which §5 lists as required. The history, read in git: the first version (aa1ef5b, 2026-05-20) said "SHOULD include"; #12 (8702057, 2026-06-02 18:36 +0200) changed §5 to "MUST include" so that it matched the schemas of #11, which required all twelve members; 42 minutes later #15 (f79bb14) made node_id, request and property optional in the schema again, calling them "SHOULD-level", without changing §5 and without a record. Spec and schema have disagreed since.

ADR numbers 0010 and 0011 are not used. ADR 0007 and 0008 (#147) replace two records the reference implementation kept under those numbers, which this repository cited for months; a record of this repository with the same number would make those citations ambiguous.

Decision

D1. The stay asked for (§7). A new condition: request.check_in, request.check_out and request.guests are the dates and the guest count the agent asked for. A verifier that does not know what was asked for (a pasted offer, for example) cannot meet it.

D2. Dates (§5, §7). check_in and check_out MUST be dates that exist (RFC 3339 full-date), and check_out MUST be later than check_in. A node MUST NOT sign an offer for dates that break this, or whose check_in is earlier than the current date at the property; it answers with an error. A verifier does not know the property's time zone, so §7 asks it for less: check_in not earlier than the day before its current UTC date. The local date anywhere is at most one day behind the UTC date, so a check_in earlier than that has passed everywhere. The floor lets through a check_in that passed at the property up to a day earlier; the node's rule is what stops it. Dates that do not exist (30 February, 31 April) are a runtime rule: no pattern can stop them.

D3. Echoed fields (§5, §7). check_in, check_out, guests and language in request are the requester's words, not statements by the host; check_in_weekday, check_out_weekday and nights, which the known production node also signs, are the node's own, computed from the dates. A node MUST NOT copy into the signed payload any value from the request other than those four. request.language, when present, MUST be a BCP 47 language tag of the form language subtag (two or three letters), optional script subtag (four letters), optional region subtag (two letters or three digits), in any letter case, and SHOULD name a language the node writes the offer in. The schema's pattern ends in $(?!\n), so that an engine whose $ also matches before a final line break (Python's re, which some JSON Schema validators use) does not accept "sv\n"; a vector holds that case. Agents MUST NOT present a value from request as something the host stated.

D4. Lifetime (§5, §6, §3.1). A node MUST set generated_at to the time it signs. valid_until MUST be later than generated_at and MUST NOT be more than 900 seconds after it, the two values compared as written, fractions of a second included. An agent MUST treat an offer as not quotable when generated_at is missing or malformed, more than 60 seconds later than its own clock, or when the lifetime is not positive or exceeds 900 seconds. 900 seconds is the known production node's lifetime. The 60 seconds allow for clocks out of step: at worst an offer stays quotable for 960 seconds by the verifier's clock. §3.1's "maximum offer lifetime" now names the 900 seconds.

D5. Schema. offerPayload.required gains request. $defs/date admits months 01–12 and days 01–31 only, $defs/dateTime hours 00–23 and minutes and seconds 00–59; that a date exists (no 30 February) stays a runtime rule. These patterns and the D3 pattern end in $(?!\n), for the reason D3 gives. request.language gets the D3 pattern. offerPayload.required also gains node_id and property, which returns the schema to what §5 and #11 say; property itself still requires only property_id.

D6. Conformance vectors and the site verifier. examples/conformance/offer-rules/vectors.v0.1.json (44 cases: lifetime and clock 16, dates 12, language 9, the stay asked for 6, plus one offer that meets every rule), built by scripts/offer-rules-vectors.mjs and checked by scripts/verify-offer-rules.mjs in npm test. scripts/validate-artifacts.mjs gains five negative schema checks (no request, no node_id, no property, a free-text language, month 13). verify.html applies D1–D4 to a live offer, with the dates and guest count typed into the page as the stay asked for.

Consequences

  • Version class. D1–D5 change what a verifier accepts, a MAJOR-class change under GOVERNANCE.md §5, made within the v0.1 public draft under its "Before 1.0" paragraph (ADR 0009 D6). What stops being accepted: offers for another stay than the one asked for; dates that do not exist, a check_out not later than check_in, a check_in that has passed; a request.language that is not a language tag; a lifetime that is not positive or exceeds 900 seconds; a generated_at more than 60 seconds ahead; an offer without request, node_id or property.
  • Known nodes affected: the known production node. Measured 2026-10-17 before its change (18:13 UTC): its ordinary offers (a future stay, without a language and with sv) met every rule and validated against the changed schema, and it also signed offers for a check_in that had passed, for month 13, and with free text as request.language. It changed in its own repository. Measured after that change was live (18:44 UTC): those requests are answered 400 (checkin_in_past, invalid_date), free text is left out of request, and ordinary offers are as before, with a 900-second lifetime. The condition this record set, that change live and measured before acceptance, is met.
  • Substantive under GOVERNANCE.md §4 and §12: this record, seven days of review and two approvals while two maintainers are active.
  • Migration path. A node validates the dates, refuses a past check_in by the property's own date, copies only the four request members, sends a language tag or nothing, and keeps the lifetime at 900 seconds or less with generated_at at signing time. A verifier passes the stay it asked for to its check and applies the date, language and lifetime rules. vacationrentalprotocol/vrp-verify and the reference implementation follow in their own repositories.
  • The examples in this repository already meet every rule: their lifetime is 600 seconds, their stays lie after their clocks, and none carries request.language. No signed example changes.
  • The IETF draft keeps the old wording until its next revision.
  • Neutrality: the rules apply to every node and every verifier alike, the reference implementation included (ADR 0002).
