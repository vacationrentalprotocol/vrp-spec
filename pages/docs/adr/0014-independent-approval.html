ADR 0014: Independent Approval of Substantive Changes

Status: Proposed Date: 2026-11-04 (proposed)

Related: GOVERNANCE.md §4, §8, §12, MAINTAINERS.md, ADR 0002, ADR 0003, ADR 0015, ADR 0016.

This record is itself a substantive change (GOVERNANCE.md §12). It is in force only after at least seven days of review with no maintainer objecting and approval from two maintainers that counts on its current head (D3), and after it is merged (§4). Until then GOVERNANCE.md as it reads on main governs, and nothing in this record applies.

Context

GOVERNANCE.md §4 requires, once two maintainers are active, "approval from two maintainers" for a substantive change (read at 5db83f4, lines 81–86). It does not say which two. MAINTAINERS.md discloses that the lead maintainer and the project-continuity maintainer "are not independent of each other" (lines 30–32), and that the third maintainer, an independent implementer, was added on 2026-10-23. Under the text as written, a substantive change can therefore be approved by two maintainers who are not independent of each other, and merged by one of them.

The rule applies to every substantive change, including one that would weaken Core v0.1 §2 (a node is a domain registered for the host, never a name under another party's domain). The rule as written does not protect that section, or any other, against a change approved inside one organization.

This is the pattern the larger standards bodies avoid. RFC 6410 §2.2 (read 2026-11-04): a document advances "in an IETF-wide Last Call of at least four weeks" confirmed by the IESG, not by its authors, and only when "there are at least two independent interoperating implementations". The W3C Process Document (18 August 2025, §6.3.2, read 2026-11-04) asks, before a specification advances, "are there implementations created by people other than the authors of the specification?". In both, someone other than the author has to say yes.

The practice has been better than the text: pull request #178 (ADR 0013) was approved by the independent maintainer on 2026-11-01 11:26:27 UTC and by the continuity maintainer on 2026-11-02 06:00:50 UTC (read from the pull request's timeline). This record writes that practice into the rule, so that it holds whoever is at the table.

Decision

D1. One independent approval. Of the two approvals GOVERNANCE.md §4 requires for a substantive change, at least one MUST come from a maintainer who is independent of the change's author. The author's own approval never counts toward the two.

D2. Independence. Two maintainers are independent of each other when neither is employed by, contracted to, an owner of, or a family member of the other or of the other's organization. MAINTAINERS.md records, for each maintainer, the organizations and maintainers they are not independent of, as it does today in its "Disclosure" section. A maintainer who is unsure declares the relationship; a declared relationship counts as non-independence.

D3. Merge. The maintainer who merges a substantive change MUST be one of its approvers or the lead maintainer, and MUST NOT merge before the review period has run and both approvals count on the current head. When the review period starts, and when it starts again, is set by ADR 0015 D2–D3; this record sets no rule of its own for that.

An approval counts on the head it was given on. It counts on a later head only when all of the following hold:

  1. every commit added after the approved head is a merge of main into the change, made without resolving a conflict and without any other addition;
  2. the content and meaning of the change under review are unchanged: what the change adds, alters or removes compared with main is the same at the new head as at the approved head;
  3. the checks pass on the new head;
  4. the approval has not been withdrawn or dismissed.

The maintainer who merges records the comparison in the pull request: the approved commit, the new head, and that conditions 1–4 hold. Changed content, a resolved conflict, or doubt about any condition means the change needs new approvals on the current head. An approval that has been withdrawn or dismissed never counts. The removal of a part marked at risk does not restart the review period (ADR 0015 D3); that is a rule about the period, and it does not by itself make an earlier approval count.

D4. When no independent maintainer is available. If no maintainer independent of the author is active (GOVERNANCE.md §8), a substantive change waits. It is not approved by two non-independent maintainers in the meantime. Restoring an independent maintainer follows §8.

D5. GOVERNANCE.md text. On acceptance, §4's bullet "Two approvals once two maintainers are active" gains the sentence: "At least one of the two approvals comes from a maintainer independent of the change's author, as ADR 0014 defines independence; the author's own approval does not count." §12 is unchanged.

Consequences

  • No substantive change, including a change to Core v0.1 §2, can be approved inside one organization. This holds for the reference implementer as for any other operator (ADR 0002): the rule names no company and favours none.
  • The lead maintainer keeps the merge and the editorial role; what changes is who must have said yes first.
  • The project depends on having at least one independent maintainer active. That is the state since 2026-10-23 (MAINTAINERS.md). GOVERNANCE.md §8 is the path to more.
  • Editorial and non-breaking changes are unchanged (GOVERNANCE.md §4): one maintainer approves and merges, and that maintainer may be the change's author.
  • This record, ADR 0015 and ADR 0016 are approved under the rule they describe: the independent maintainer's approval is one of the two for each of them.
